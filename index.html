This ranks bets by how far a book's price sits from the sharp consensus — not by who is likely to win. It uses no projection of your own. For every game it de-vigs the median of all other books, backs out the number that implies, then re-prices each book at its own line. A stale or generous book surfaces as +EV. Needs at least 4 books on a market to mean anything.

Read this before betting off it A price better than consensus is a real edge, but a small one — expect 1–3%, not 20%. It also decays fast: stale lines get corrected, often within minutes. And a single book alone against the field is sometimes right rather than slow, especially on news the others haven't priced yet. Check why it's an outlier before you fire.
Weekly slate planner

Every stake elsewhere in this app is sized as though that bet is your only exposure. It isn't. This sizes the whole card at once — solving for the stakes that maximise expected log growth across all of them together — and resolves bets on the same game from a single simulated game, so a team's spread and its moneyline correlate properly instead of pretending to be independent. Uses your bankroll and Kelly fraction from the Calibrator tab.

Odds API

Uses the-odds-api.com. The free tier is 500 credits/month, and the /odds endpoint bills credits = markets × regions — so three markets across US books costs 3 credits per league, not 1. Fetch only the markets you actually price. Your key is stored in this browser's localStorage only; it is never sent anywhere except the odds API itself.

Making 500 credits last a season Slates are cached in this browser, so reopening the app or reloading the page costs nothing — only "Fetch live odds" spends credits. Pull a slate once, work the whole card off it, and refresh only when you need current numbers. At spreads + totals that's 4 credits for both leagues, or about 125 full scans a month.

Model parameters Standard deviations used to turn your projection into a probability
1 = treat all books equally
fee ≈ k × P × (1−P) per contract
Why exchanges need a fee model An event contract carries no vig, so de-vigging one finds almost no hold — because there isn't any. The cost arrives as a separate trading fee instead. Left unmodelled, every Kalshi price would look like a free 2–3% edge and swamp the board. At k = 0.07 the fee peaks near 1.75¢ per contract at even money, still well inside a sportsbook's hold. Verify the current figure against your platform — Polymarket and Novig default to zero here.
What the sharp weight does Sharp books count this many times a retail book when the consensus is formed. At 4, two sharp books outweigh seven retail ones — which is deliberate, since they make the number rather than copy it, but it does mean the consensus becomes close to “what Pinnacle and LowVig think.” Drop it toward 2 to let the wider market have a say, or to 1 for a flat median.
Why these differ College games have fatter margin tails and a softer market, so a situational read is worth more points there. Don't raise the max lean much past these values — that's how a discipline tool turns into a rationalization tool.

Gridiron Edge is a decision-support tool. It measures whether a price beats your own projection — it does not predict outcomes, and its most common correct answer is "pass."
